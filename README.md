
# Moodle Learning Ecosystem Design-for-Change Toolkit

![alt text](https://github.com/hernandez-jc/Design-for-Change-Consulting-Toolkit/blob/main/assets/Moodle-ecosystem-image.jpg)


A compact, consultative toolkit for assessing a Moodle learning ecosystem, identifying practical improvement priorities, and producing a manageable 10-day roadmap.

This project is designed for Moodle Learning Designers, LMS administrators, L&D consultants, and small implementation teams who need a simple way to move from discovery to action without overcomplicating the process.

## Purpose

This toolkit helps an organization:

1. Describe the purpose, users, business goals, and learning outcomes of its Moodle learning ecosystem.
2. Assess the current strategy, learners, content, administration, data, accessibility, integrations, and governance.
3. Identify barriers to learner adoption, administrator usability, content quality, and organizational change.
4. Establish constraints and prioritize improvements based on impact, effort, risk, and urgency.
5. Produce a practical 10-day roadmap with owners, deliverables, dependencies, and success measures.

The toolkit is intentionally limited in scope. It does not attempt to redesign an entire organization’s learning strategy in one project. Instead, it provides a repeatable first cycle: understand, assess, prioritize, plan, and review.

## Who it is for

|---|---|
| Moodle Learning Designer | Facilitates discovery, assesses learning design, and prepares recommendations. |
| L&D or Training Manager | Defines business goals, learner needs, priorities, and success measures. |

## Business goals

The toolkit supports organizations that want to:

- Improve the usefulness and adoption of their Moodle learning environment.
- Align learning activities with real work tasks and organizational priorities.
- Reduce duplicated content, unclear navigation, and administrative burden.
- Improve accessibility and inclusion for diverse learners.
- Make better use of Moodle’s core reporting and analytics capabilities.
- Identify practical improvements that can be delivered within a short, controlled timeframe.
- Create a foundation for ongoing, evidence-informed improvement.

## Learning outcomes

After using this toolkit, a project team should be able to:

- Describe the purpose, audience, business goals, and learning outcomes of the Moodle ecosystem.
- Complete a basic assessment of strategy, learners, content, administration, data, accessibility, integrations, and governance.
- Identify the most important barriers affecting learners, administrators, content quality, and change readiness.
- Prioritize improvements using impact, effort, risk, and urgency.
- Produce a realistic 10-day action plan with clear ownership and success measures.
- Use the results as the basis for a future improvement cycle.

## Target audience

This toolkit is designed for:

- Organizations using Moodle LMS or MoodleCloud.
- Teams that want a practical first assessment rather than a large-scale transformation project.
- Organizations that need to improve learner adoption, administrator usability, content quality, or learning governance.
- Teams that prefer core Moodle capabilities and simple, editable templates.
- Consultants or Learning Designers who need a structured discovery and planning resource for client work.

## Scope

### In scope

- Basic discovery and stakeholder input.
- Current-state assessment of the Moodle learning ecosystem.
- Identification of adoption, usability, content, and governance barriers.
- Prioritization of improvements.
- A 10-day roadmap with owners, deliverables, dependencies, and success measures.
- A short executive summary for decision-makers.

### Out of scope

- Full organizational change management programs.
- Custom plugin development.
- Migration from another LMS.
- Detailed technical architecture design.
- Comprehensive data-protection legal review.
- Moodle Workplace implementation.
- Large-scale content redevelopment.

## Guiding principles

- **Start with the work:** Understand what learners and the organization need to accomplish before recommending tools or courses.
- **Assess before adding:** Identify what already works, what is missing, and what creates friction.
- **Prioritize practical value:** Focus on improvements that are meaningful, feasible, and visible to learners and administrators.
- **Design for change:** Prefer modular, reusable, and adaptable structures over rigid one-off solutions.
- **Protect learner and organizational data:** Clarify ownership, access, retention, and appropriate use of data.
- **Design for inclusion:** Treat accessibility, captions, transcripts, clear navigation, and mobile access as foundational requirements.
- **Keep governance visible:** Define who decides, who maintains, who reviews, and who approves changes.
- **Use evidence:** Base recommendations on stakeholder input, Moodle reports, learner feedback, and observed barriers.

## How to use it

1. Start with the **Project Charter** to define purpose, users, business goals, learning outcomes, scope, and constraints.
2. Use the **Client Discovery** guide to collect input from key stakeholders.
3. Complete the **Learning Checkup Scorecard** to assess the current Moodle ecosystem.
4. Record barriers and constraints in the **Barriers and Constraints** template.
5. Prioritize findings using the **Prioritization Matrix**.
6. Create a realistic **10-Day Roadmap**.
7. Summarize findings and recommendations in the **Executive Brief**.

Each document is designed to be used independently, but they follow the same project logic:

```text
Purpose and goals → Current-state assessment → Barriers and constraints → Priorities → 10-day roadmap → Executive brief
```

## 10-day roadmap approach

The roadmap is deliberately short and manageable. It is not intended to solve every issue identified during discovery.

Instead, it should focus on a small number of practical improvements that can be completed or meaningfully advanced within 10 working days.

A good 10-day roadmap includes:

- A clear objective.
- A limited number of priorities.
- One accountable owner per deliverable.
- Dependencies and assumptions.
- Simple success measures.
- A review point at the end of the cycle.
