# 02 — Client Discovery

This guide provides a compact set of discovery questions for understanding the organization’s Moodle learning ecosystem before assessing it or preparing a 10-day improvement roadmap.

It follows the project charter and focuses on the eight assessment areas:

1. Strategy and business alignment  
2. Learners and audiences  
3. Content and learning design  
4. Administration and usability  
5. Data and reporting  
6. Accessibility and inclusion  
7. Integrations and interoperability  
8. Governance and change readiness  

Use the questions selectively. The goal is to gather enough evidence to identify practical priorities—not to conduct an exhaustive audit.

---

## How to use this guide

1. Confirm the project sponsor, project lead, and required participants.
2. Schedule short discovery conversations with the relevant stakeholders.
3. Use the questions most relevant to each participant.
4. Record answers, evidence, examples, and unresolved questions.
5. Identify recurring themes, barriers, constraints, and possible quick wins.
6. Carry the findings into the Learning Checkup Scorecard and Barriers and Constraints log.

A practical discovery cycle can be completed through:

- One kickoff conversation with the business sponsor.
- One conversation with the L&D or Training Manager.
- One conversation with the Moodle Administrator.
- One conversation with IT or the systems contact.
- One short learner or manager feedback activity.

---

## Discovery principles

- **Start with purpose:** Understand what the organization and learners need before discussing tools or courses.
- **Listen for work context:** Ask what people need to do, decide, learn, or improve in their roles.
- **Look for evidence:** Ask for examples, reports, screenshots, navigation paths, and recent feedback.
- **Separate facts from opinions:** Record what is observed, what is reported, and what still needs verification.
- **Identify constraints early:** Time, access, skills, policy, data, and technical dependencies affect what can be improved.
- **Keep the scope manageable:** Focus on issues that can inform a realistic 10-day roadmap.

## 1. Strategy and business alignment

**Purpose:** Understand why the Moodle ecosystem exists and what business outcomes it should support.

### Questions for the business sponsor

- What business problem or opportunity should the Moodle learning ecosystem support?
- What organizational priorities depend on people learning new knowledge, skills, or behaviors?
- What must improve in the next 3 to 6 months?
- What does success look like for learners, managers, and the organization?
- Which learning programs are most important right now?
- Are there upcoming changes that may affect learning needs, such as new systems, policies, roles, regulations, or technology?
- What constraints should the project respect?

### Questions for L&D or Training

- What are the main learning goals of the current Moodle environment?
- Which audiences or job roles are the highest priority?
- What learning is mandatory, role-based, or optional?
- How is learning aligned with job tasks, performance expectations, or career development?
- What learning is working well today?
- Where do learners or managers report confusion, low engagement, or low completion?
- What has already been tried to improve the learning environment?

### Evidence to request

- Current learning strategy or training plan.
- List of priority audiences, roles, or departments.
- Examples of mandatory, role-based, and optional learning.
- Recent feedback from learners, managers, or facilitators.
- Known business events, system changes, or compliance deadlines.

---

## 2. Learners and audiences

**Purpose:** Understand who uses the Moodle environment, what they need to accomplish, and what may prevent them from participating effectively.

### Questions

- Who are the main learner groups?
- What roles, locations, languages, devices, or work schedules affect learning access?
- What do learners need to know, do, or decide after completing key learning?
- How do learners currently find the learning they need?
- What parts of the Moodle environment do learners use most often?
- Where do learners get stuck, lose interest, or stop participating?
- What feedback have learners provided about navigation, relevance, workload, or clarity?
- Are there groups with specific accessibility, language, connectivity, or scheduling needs?

### Evidence to request

- Learner groups, departments, or role categories.
- Enrolment numbers or audience estimates.
- Examples of learner feedback, support requests, or completion patterns.
- Common devices and connection conditions.
- Examples of learning that learners find useful or difficult to access.

---

## 3. Content and learning design

**Purpose:** Assess whether learning content is relevant, current, clear, accessible, and aligned with learning outcomes.

### Questions

- What types of learning content exist in Moodle?
- Which courses, Moodle Books, activities, quizzes, SCORM packages, or resources are most important?
- How is learning content organized: by role, topic, compliance requirement, department, or another structure?
- Are learning outcomes clear for the most important programs?
- Does content connect to real tasks, decisions, or workplace situations?
- How current is the most important content?
- Are there duplicated, outdated, unused, or inconsistent learning materials?
- How is feedback provided to learners?
- Are captions, transcripts, readable documents, and accessible media available where needed?

### Evidence to request

- Course catalogue or course category structure.
- Examples of priority courses or learning pathways.
- Course completion or activity reports, where available.
- Examples of outdated, duplicated, or unused content.
- Accessibility examples, including captioned videos or transcripts.

---

## 4. Administration and usability

**Purpose:** Understand whether administrators can manage the Moodle environment efficiently and whether the site is easy for learners and administrators to use.

### Questions for the Moodle Administrator

- How is the Moodle site currently structured?
- How are users created, updated, and deactivated?
- Which authentication and enrolment methods are used?
- How are roles and permissions managed?
- How are groups and cohorts used?
- What manual administrative tasks take the most time?
- What recurring problems do administrators or teachers report?
- Which reports are used most often?
- What site settings, navigation, or branding issues affect usability?
- What would make day-to-day administration easier?

### Questions for teachers or facilitators

- What is easy or difficult about building and maintaining courses?
- What Moodle activities or resources do you use most often?
- Where do you spend unnecessary time?
- What learner questions or issues occur repeatedly?
- What improvements would help you support learners more effectively?

### Evidence to request

- Site category structure.
- Role and enrolment overview.
- Examples of recurring administrative tasks.
- Common support requests or troubleshooting issues.
- Examples of reports currently used for monitoring or decision-making.

---

## 5. Data and reporting

**Purpose:** Understand whether the organization has the information needed to monitor learning, identify barriers, and improve decisions.

### Questions

- What learning data is currently reviewed?
- Who reviews learning data, and how often?
- Which reports are most useful for managers, L&D, and administrators?
- What questions should learning data answer?
- Are completion, participation, assessment, or engagement data easy to interpret?
- Are there data quality issues, such as duplicate accounts, incomplete records, or inconsistent course naming?
- What data is needed but not currently available?
- Are there restrictions on who can access learner or learning data?

### Evidence to request

- Examples of reports currently used.
- Definitions of key measures, such as enrolment, completion, participation, or assessment.
- Examples of data quality issues.
- Roles or teams that require access to learning data.

---

## 6. Accessibility and inclusion

**Purpose:** Identify whether learners can access and participate in learning without unnecessary barriers.

### Questions

- Are learners able to access Moodle on the devices and connections they use for work?
- Are videos captioned and important audio or video content supported by transcripts?
- Are documents readable, well structured, and usable with assistive technology?
- Is navigation clear and consistent across courses?
- Are learning materials available in the languages learners need?
- Are there learners who need additional support to participate fully?
- What accessibility issues have been reported or observed?
- How are accessibility requirements checked before content is published?

### Evidence to request

- Examples of courses, videos, documents, or quizzes to review.
- Learner feedback related to access, readability, language, or device use.
- Current accessibility guidance or review practices.
- Examples of captioned videos, transcripts, or accessible documents.

---

## 7. Integrations and interoperability

**Purpose:** Understand how Moodle connects—or may need to connect—with other systems, while maintaining appropriate control over data and access.

### Questions

- Which systems currently connect with Moodle, if any?
- Which systems may need to connect in the future?
- How are user identities, organizational units, roles, or enrolments managed across systems?
- What data is exchanged between systems?
- Who owns each data source?
- Who can approve access to learner or learning data?
- Are there existing constraints related to privacy, security, data residency, or retention?
- Are there manual processes that could be simplified through a controlled integration?

### Evidence to request

- List of current or planned systems, such as HR, CRM, identity provider, student information system, or analytics tools.
- Data-flow overview, if available.
- Existing integration requirements or restrictions.
- Names of data owners and approval contacts.

---

## 8. Governance and change readiness

**Purpose:** Understand whether ownership, maintenance, decision-making, and change processes are clear enough to support a sustainable learning ecosystem.

### Questions

- Who owns the Moodle learning ecosystem?
- Who approves new courses, content, users, roles, or integrations?
- Who maintains and updates learning content?
- How are content reviews scheduled?
- How are learner feedback, support requests, and reports used to improve the environment?
- How are changes communicated to learners, teachers, and administrators?
- What has made previous changes difficult to adopt?
- What would help the organization adapt more easily to future changes?

### Evidence to request

- Names or roles responsible for Moodle ownership and maintenance.
- Existing approval, review, or publication processes.
- Examples of recent changes and how they were communicated.
- Known barriers to previous technology or learning changes.

## Output of discovery

At the end of discovery, the project team should have:

- A clear statement of business goals and learning outcomes.
- A defined list of priority learner audiences.
- Initial evidence about the current Moodle ecosystem.
- A list of observed or reported barriers.
- A list of constraints, dependencies, and risks.
- Initial candidates for quick wins and longer-term improvements.
- Input required for the Learning Checkup Scorecard.
