# 01 Project Charter

This charter defines the purpose, users, business goals, learning outcomes, scope, constraints, and success measures for a focused Moodle learning ecosystem review.

It is the first document in the **Moodle Learning Ecosystem Design-for-Change Toolkit**. It establishes the boundaries for the discovery, assessment, prioritization, and 10-day roadmap activities that follow.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this project is to help an organization understand the current state of its Moodle learning ecosystem and identify a small number of practical improvements that can be initiated within 10 working days.

The project does not attempt to redesign every course, process, or system. Instead, it provides a structured first cycle to:

- Clarify the purpose of the Moodle learning ecosystem.
- Understand the people it serves and the work it supports.
- Assess the current learning strategy, content, administration, data, accessibility, integrations, and governance.
- Identify barriers to learner adoption, administrator usability, content quality, and organizational change.
- Prioritize improvements using impact, effort, risk, and urgency.
- Produce a realistic 10-day action plan.

This approach follows the principle of **design for change**: a Moodle environment should be adaptable, understandable, and able to evolve as learner needs, business priorities, technologies, and organizational requirements change.

---

## Project objective

Produce a practical, evidence-informed **10-day Moodle improvement roadmap** based on a focused assessment of the organization’s current learning ecosystem.

The roadmap should identify:

- What is working well.
- What needs improvement.
- Which barriers are most important.
- Which improvements should be addressed first.
- Who is responsible for each action.
- What dependencies may affect delivery.
- How success will be measured.

---

## Business goals

The organization should select or confirm the business goals most relevant to its context. Typical goals include:

| Business goal | What the Moodle ecosystem should support |
|---|---|
| Improve learner adoption | Make learning easier to find, understand, access, and complete. |
| Support workforce capability | Connect learning to real tasks, role expectations, and performance needs. |
| Improve operational efficiency | Reduce duplicated content, unclear processes, and administrative burden. |
| Strengthen compliance and governance | Make required learning visible, trackable, current, and appropriately controlled. |
| Improve accessibility and inclusion | Remove barriers to participation for diverse learners and work contexts. |
| Improve decision-making | Use Moodle reports and learner feedback to inform practical improvements. |
| Prepare for change | Create modular, reusable learning structures that can adapt to new priorities. |

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this project, the project team should be able to:

1. Describe the purpose, users, business goals, and learning outcomes of the Moodle learning ecosystem.
2. Assess the current strategy, learners, content, administration, data, accessibility, integrations, and governance.
3. Identify barriers to learner adoption, administrator usability, content quality, and organizational change.
4. Establish constraints and prioritize improvements based on impact, effort, risk, and urgency.
5. Produce a practical 10-day roadmap with owners, deliverables, dependencies, and success measures.

## Scope

### In scope

This project includes:

- A short discovery process with selected stakeholders.
- A basic assessment of the Moodle learning ecosystem.
- Review of learner adoption, administrator usability, content quality, and change readiness.
- Identification of constraints, risks, and dependencies.
- Prioritization of improvements using impact, effort, risk, and urgency.
- Development of a 10-day roadmap.
- Preparation of a concise executive brief.

### Out of scope

This project does not include:

- A full organizational change-management program.
- Complete redesign of all courses or learning content.
- Custom plugin development.
- LMS migration.
- Detailed technical architecture design.
- Legal review of privacy, data protection, or regulatory obligations.
- Moodle Workplace implementation.
- Procurement of new platforms or major systems.

---

## Assessment areas

The project assesses the Moodle learning ecosystem across eight areas:

| Area | Core question |
|---|---|
| **Strategy and business alignment** | Does the Moodle ecosystem support clear business and learning goals? |
| **Learners and audiences** | Are learner needs, roles, access requirements, and work contexts understood? |
| **Content and learning design** | Is learning relevant, current, accessible, engaging, and aligned with outcomes? |
| **Administration and usability** | Can administrators manage users, enrolment, roles, content, and reporting efficiently? |
| **Data and reporting** | Are the right data available to understand usage, progress, and improvement needs? |
| **Accessibility and inclusion** | Can diverse learners access and participate in learning without unnecessary barriers? |
| **Integrations and interoperability** | Are connections with other systems clear, controlled, and appropriate? |
| **Governance and change readiness** | Are ownership, decision-making, maintenance, and change processes defined? |

---

## Constraints

The project should explicitly record constraints before prioritizing improvements. Common constraints include:

- Limited time, budget, or staffing.
- Limited administrator capacity.
- Existing content that cannot be changed immediately.
- Technical limitations of the current Moodle environment.
- Data access, privacy, security, or approval requirements.
- Dependencies on HR, IT, CRM, identity, or reporting systems.
- Organizational policies, compliance requirements, or contractual obligations.
- Need to avoid disruption to active learning programs.

Constraints should be treated as design inputs, not obstacles. They help determine which improvements are realistic within the 10-day roadmap.

---

## Prioritization criteria

Improvements are prioritized using four criteria:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Impact** | How much will this improve learner experience, adoption, usability, quality, or business value? |
| **Effort** | How much time, skill, coordination, or technical work is required? |
| **Risk** | What could go wrong if this issue is not addressed, or if the change is implemented poorly? |
| **Urgency** | How soon does this need to be addressed to support learners, operations, compliance, or upcoming change? |

Use these criteria to distinguish between:

- **Quick wins:** High impact, low effort, low risk.
- **Foundational improvements:** Important for long-term adaptability, but requiring more coordination.
- **Deferred items:** Valid concerns that should be scheduled for a later cycle.
- **Out-of-scope items:** Issues that require a separate project, budget, or decision.

## Success measures

Success should be measured against the project objective, not against the total number of findings.

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Project charter completed and approved | Yes |
| Key stakeholders consulted | At least the required roles identified in this charter |
| Learning checkup completed | All eight assessment areas reviewed |
| Barriers and constraints documented | Yes |
| Improvement priorities agreed | Yes |
| 10-day roadmap produced | Yes |
| Owners and success measures defined | Yes |
| Executive brief prepared | Yes |

A successful outcome is a short, realistic, and actionable roadmap—not an exhaustive list of every possible improvement.

---

## Assumptions

This charter assumes that:

- The organization uses Moodle LMS or MoodleCloud.
- Core Moodle capabilities are available, and no third-party plugins are required for this initial review.
- Key stakeholders can provide input within the project timeframe.
- The organization wants a practical first improvement cycle, not a full transformation program.
- Findings will be reviewed and prioritized before implementation.
- The 10-day roadmap is a planning and initiation cycle, not a guarantee that every identified issue will be resolved.
