# 04 — Barriers and Constraints

This page converts the findings from the Learning Checkup Scorecard into a clear record of barriers, constraints, risks, and dependencies.

Its purpose is to help the project team understand what may prevent learners, administrators, content, or the organization from achieving the intended learning outcomes.

Use the findings from **[03 — Learning Checkup Scorecard](docs/03-learning-checkup-scorecard.md)** to complete this page.

---

## Purpose

A barrier is something that makes it harder for learners, administrators, or the organization to use the Moodle learning ecosystem effectively.

A constraint is a limitation that affects what can be improved, how quickly it can be improved, or who must be involved.

Recording both separately helps the team distinguish between:

- Problems that can be addressed through learning design or Moodle configuration.
- Limitations that must be respected when planning improvements.
- Risks that require monitoring or escalation.
- Dependencies that may affect delivery.

---

## How to use this page

1. Review the gaps identified in the Learning Checkup Scorecard.
2. Describe each barrier in practical, specific language.
3. Identify who or what is affected.
4. Record the evidence or example that supports the finding.
5. Identify the likely impact on learners, administrators, content quality, or business goals.
6. Record constraints, risks, and dependencies separately.
7. Select the most important items for prioritization in the next page.

Avoid recording every possible issue. Focus on findings that are relevant to the project objective and the 10-day roadmap.

## Barrier categories

Use these categories to keep the analysis focused and consistent.

| Category | Core question |
|---|---|
| **Learner adoption** | What prevents learners from finding, accessing, understanding, or completing learning? |
| **Administrator usability** | What makes it difficult for administrators or teachers to manage and maintain the Moodle environment? |
| **Content quality** | What reduces the relevance, clarity, currency, consistency, or accessibility of learning content? |
| **Organizational change** | What makes it difficult for the organization to sustain or adapt the learning ecosystem? |

---

## 1. Learner adoption barriers

Learner adoption barriers affect whether learners can participate in learning successfully.

| Barrier type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Findability** | Learners cannot easily find relevant courses or resources. |
| **Navigation** | Course structure, labels, or categories are unclear. |
| **Access** | Learners cannot access Moodle from their usual device, location, or work context. |
| **Relevance** | Learning does not clearly connect to the learner’s role or work tasks. |
| **Clarity** | Learning outcomes, instructions, or expectations are unclear. |
| **Workload** | Learning takes too long or creates unnecessary cognitive load. |
| **Motivation** | Learners do not understand the value or purpose of the learning. |
| **Language or accessibility** | Content, navigation, media, or documents create barriers for some learners. |

### Prompts

- What prevents learners from starting or completing priority learning?
- Where do learners get stuck or ask for help most often?
- Which learning is difficult to find, understand, or apply?
- What feedback have learners provided about usability, relevance, or access?

---

## 2. Administrator usability barriers

Administrator usability barriers affect the people who build, maintain, support, and report on the Moodle environment.

| Barrier type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Site structure** | Course categories, naming conventions, or navigation are inconsistent. |
| **User management** | Creating, updating, or deactivating users requires repeated manual work. |
| **Enrolment** | Enrolment methods are unclear, inconsistent, or difficult to maintain. |
| **Roles and permissions** | It is unclear who can do what, or permissions create unnecessary restrictions. |
| **Groups and cohorts** | Groups or cohorts are not used consistently to organize learners. |
| **Content maintenance** | Updating or reviewing content is time-consuming or unclear. |
| **Reporting** | Reports are difficult to find, interpret, or use for decisions. |
| **Support requests** | Administrators receive repeated questions about the same issues. |

### Prompts

- Which administrative tasks take the most time?
- What recurring problems do administrators or teachers report?
- Which Moodle settings, roles, or enrolment practices create confusion?
- What would make daily administration easier?

---

## 3. Content quality barriers

Content quality barriers affect whether learning is relevant, current, clear, accessible, and aligned with learning outcomes.

| Barrier type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Unclear outcomes** | Learners do not understand what they should know or be able to do. |
| **Low relevance** | Content does not connect to real tasks, decisions, or workplace needs. |
| **Outdated content** | Policies, systems, examples, links, or procedures have changed. |
| **Duplication** | Similar content exists in multiple places without a clear source of truth. |
| **Inconsistent structure** | Courses use different layouts, naming conventions, or activity patterns. |
| **Weak assessment or feedback** | Learners cannot confirm understanding or receive useful guidance. |
| **Accessibility gaps** | Videos lack captions, documents are difficult to read, or media is not usable by all learners. |
| **Poor mobile experience** | Content is difficult to use on phones, tablets, or limited connections. |

### Prompts

- Which priority content is outdated, duplicated, or difficult to use?
- Where is learning not clearly connected to job tasks or learning outcomes?
- What content creates repeated learner questions or support requests?
- Which accessibility issues have been observed or reported?

---

## 4. Organizational change barriers

Organizational change barriers affect whether improvements can be sustained and adopted over time.

| Barrier type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Unclear ownership** | No one is clearly responsible for the Moodle ecosystem or its content. |
| **Unclear decision-making** | It is not clear who approves courses, users, roles, or changes. |
| **Limited capacity** | Administrators, teachers, or L&D staff do not have enough time. |
| **Low awareness** | Stakeholders do not understand what Moodle can do or how to use it. |
| **Resistance to change** | Users prefer existing processes or do not see the value of the change. |
| **Poor communication** | Changes are not explained clearly or at the right time. |
| **Unclear maintenance** | Content reviews, updates, and support responsibilities are not defined. |
| **Changing priorities** | Business needs, systems, roles, or regulations change faster than the learning environment. |

### Prompts

- Who owns the Moodle ecosystem and its improvement?
- What has made previous changes difficult to adopt?
- What would help the organization maintain and adapt the environment?
- Which upcoming changes could affect learning needs or Moodle administration?
