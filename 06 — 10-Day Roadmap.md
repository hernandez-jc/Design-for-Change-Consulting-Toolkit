# 06 — 10-Day Roadmap

This roadmap turns the selected priorities from **[05 — Prioritization Matrix](https://github.com/hernandez-jc/Design-for-Change-Consulting-Toolkit/blob/main/05%20%E2%80%94%20Prioritization%20Matrix.md)** into a practical, manageable 10-working-day action plan.

Its purpose is to deliver or meaningfully advance a small number of improvements that support learner adoption, administrator usability, content quality, accessibility, and organizational change readiness.

This roadmap is a first improvement cycle. It is not intended to resolve every issue identified during discovery.

---

## Purpose

The 10-day roadmap helps the project team:

- Focus on the highest-value, feasible improvements.
- Assign clear ownership for each action.
- Identify dependencies before work begins.
- Define simple deliverables and success measures.
- Review progress at the end of the cycle.
- Create a foundation for future improvement cycles.

---

## Roadmap principles

- **Keep the scope small:** Select only actions that can be completed or meaningfully advanced within 10 working days.
- **Start with quick wins:** Prioritize improvements that produce visible value with manageable effort.
- **Respect constraints:** Do not commit to actions that depend on unavailable people, approvals, systems, or budget.
- **Define ownership:** Every action needs one accountable owner.
- **Make success measurable:** Each action should have a simple success measure.
- **Review and learn:** Use the end-of-cycle review to decide what should continue, change, or be deferred.

---

## Roadmap structure

The 10-day roadmap is organized into three stages:

| Stage | Days | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Prepare and confirm** | Days 1–2 | Confirm scope, owners, access, dependencies, and success measures. |
| **Deliver improvements** | Days 3–8 | Complete the selected quick wins and begin any scoped foundational improvement. |
| **Review and communicate** | Days 9–10 | Test, review, communicate changes, and document next steps. |

## Day-by-day plan

Use this plan as a guide. Adjust the timing to match the selected actions, team availability, and organizational calendar.

### Days 1–2: Prepare and confirm

| Day | Activity | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm selected actions, owners, and success measures. | Project lead | Approved action list |
| 1 | Confirm access to Moodle, reports, content, and required stakeholders. | Moodle administrator | Access confirmed |
| 2 | Review dependencies, constraints, and risks for each action. | Project lead | Updated dependency and risk notes |
| 2 | Confirm the review date and communication approach. | Business sponsor | Review meeting scheduled |

**Checkpoint:**  
By the end of Day 2, every selected action should have an owner, deliverable, dependency, success measure, and confirmed start point.

---

### Days 3–8: Deliver improvements

| Day | Activity | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Begin work on the first selected action. | Action owner | Work started |
| 4 | Continue delivery and resolve early blockers. | Action owner | Progress update |
| 5 | Complete or advance the first action. | Action owner | Draft deliverable |
| 6 | Begin or continue the second selected action. | Action owner | Work started |
| 7 | Continue delivery and test changes with affected users. | Action owner | Feedback or test notes |
| 8 | Complete or advance remaining selected actions. | Action owners | Completed or advanced deliverables |

**Checkpoint:**  
By the end of Day 8, quick wins should be complete or close to complete. Any foundational improvement should have a clearly defined first step completed.

---

### Days 9–10: Review and communicate

| Day | Activity | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Review deliverables against success measures. | Project lead | Review notes |
| 9 | Confirm what worked, what needs adjustment, and what remains outstanding. | Project team | Updated action log |
| 10 | Communicate completed changes to learners, administrators, or managers as appropriate. | Business sponsor or project lead | Communication sent |
| 10 | Document deferred items and recommendations for the next cycle. | Project lead | Next-cycle input |

**Checkpoint:**  
By the end of Day 10, the team should have a clear record of what was delivered, what remains, and what should be prioritized next.

## Success measures

Use simple measures that can be checked without creating additional administrative burden.

| Measure type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Completion** | The deliverable was completed and reviewed. |
| **Access** | Learners can find or access the improved content or navigation. |
| **Quality** | Priority content has an owner, review date, or updated structure. |
| **Accessibility** | Priority videos include captions or transcripts. |
| **Usability** | Administrators or teachers confirm that the improvement reduces confusion or repeated work. |
| **Adoption** | Learners or managers confirm that the change is clear, relevant, or easier to use. |

Avoid using complex metrics for a 10-day cycle. The goal is to confirm whether the improvement achieved its intended practical purpose.

---

## End-of-cycle review

At the end of Day 10, answer these questions:

- Which actions were completed?
- Which actions were partially completed?
- What barriers or dependencies affected delivery?
- What evidence shows that the improvement was useful?
- What feedback was received from learners, administrators, or managers?
- What should be continued in the next cycle?
- What should be changed before repeating the approach?
- Which deferred items should be considered next?
